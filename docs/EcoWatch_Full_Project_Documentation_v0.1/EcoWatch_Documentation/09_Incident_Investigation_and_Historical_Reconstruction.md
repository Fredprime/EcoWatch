# 09 — Incident Investigation and Historical Reconstruction

**Status:** Observation/case distinction and multi-source timeline principles are confirmed design directions.

## 1. Purpose

This document describes how EcoWatch should turn separate observations into a reviewable investigation without erasing their differences or overstating what they prove.

## 2. Observation versus incident

### Observation
An original report, measurement, image, dataset record or other evidence item. It has its own source, timestamp, location, method and quality limitations.

### Incident or case
An evolving investigation record that proposes that one or more observations may relate to a real-world environmental event or condition.

The distinction is essential. A case can change as evidence develops; the original observation should remain traceable.

## 3. Linking observations to a case

A reviewer or an approved automated suggestion may link observations based on:
- Geographic proximity.
- Time proximity.
- Similar environmental category.
- Similar description or visible features.
- Shared source material.
- Known watershed or drainage relationships.
- Supporting measurements or field notes.

The system should preserve the reason for each link and allow a reviewer to reject or reverse it.

## 4. Historical reconstruction workflow

1. Identify the earliest known observation or report.
2. Record the source and precision of its date and location.
3. Search suitable historical satellite imagery, measurements, maps and reports.
4. Identify earlier evidence of the relevant environmental condition.
5. Compare multiple time points and appropriate seasons.
6. Examine possible alternative explanations.
7. Compare nearby or reference areas where suitable.
8. Record the earliest evidence currently found, not an assumed exact start date.
9. Document missing periods and uncertainty.
10. Publish a timeline that distinguishes reported dates, measured dates and interpreted dates.

## 5. Timeline representation

A case timeline may include:
- Possible event start (reported or estimated).
- First known observation.
- Additional independent observations.
- Submission and server receipt dates.
- Historical image dates.
- Field inspection dates.
- Measurement dates.
- Case status changes.
- Intervention milestones.
- Verification assessments.

Each item should display its date type and source. The system should not imply that all dates have the same evidential meaning.

## 6. Reporting delay

A reporting delay can be calculated where the observation and submission dates are sufficiently precise. It may help investigators understand when information became available, but it does not prove when an environmental problem began.

For example, a report uploaded several days after a stated observation should preserve both dates. The system must not automatically search only a fixed one- or two-day period before the report; the relevant historical search window depends on the environmental process and available data.

## 7. Conflicting accounts

Different people may report different experiences of the same location. EcoWatch should preserve those differences rather than force a single narrative. A case summary may state:
- What sources agree on.
- What sources disagree about.
- Which observations are firsthand.
- Which evidence is independent.
- What remains unknown.
- What additional investigation would resolve the uncertainty.

## 8. Investigation status

Possible workflow:
- New / unreviewed.
- Initial review.
- Evidence gathering.
- Corroborated.
- Field verification required.
- Field verified.
- Referred to responsible authority.
- Under intervention.
- Monitoring recovery.
- Closed / resolved.
- Not substantiated or merged into another case.

Final status definitions, permissions and public visibility must be agreed before implementation.

## 9. Sensitive cases

Reports involving alleged wrongdoing, private property, individuals or vulnerable reporters require careful access controls. Public case summaries should not expose personal details or present allegations as proven facts. EcoWatch should not publish accusations against named people based solely on unverified submissions.

## 10. Outputs

A useful investigation record should provide:
- Case summary.
- Map and geographic context.
- Evidence list with provenance.
- Timeline.
- Possible environmental relationships.
- Evidence gaps.
- Review history.
- Current status and next action.
- Clear distinction between verified findings and hypotheses.
