# 15 — User Interface and Experience

**Status:** Multi-role experience approved in principle; detailed screens and design system open.

## 1. Design objective

Make environmental reporting and investigation usable by people with different technical backgrounds. Keep public reporting simple while giving specialists access to detailed provenance, analysis and review tools.

## 2. Main workspaces

- **Overview:** active cases, recent observations, assigned work and system notices.
- **Report/Collect:** community reporting and structured field collection.
- **Evidence Intelligence:** candidate relationships, duplicate review and evidence graph.
- **Environmental Monitoring:** maps, monitored areas, indicators and historical layers.
- **Cases/Investigations:** case summary, map, timeline, evidence, hypotheses and actions.
- **Analysis:** datasets, methods, outputs and interpretation.
- **Prioritisation:** factor summaries and review decisions.
- **Interventions:** approved actions, owners, milestones and records.
- **Verification:** baselines, follow-up measurements and outcome assessments.
- **Administration:** users, organisations, vocabularies, imports, audit and configuration.

Expose workspaces according to role and permissions. The navigation should not imply that every user has access to all functions.

## 3. User experience principles

- Mobile-first for community reporting.
- Plain language and short explanations.
- Do not require knowledge of environmental science to submit an observation.
- Support approximate dates and uncertain locations.
- Distinguish local draft, queued, submitted and received states.
- Make map-based location optional where appropriate; provide non-map alternatives.
- Label machine-generated content distinctly.
- Show why information is requested.
- Provide accessible contrast, keyboard navigation and readable layouts.
- Use language support based on consultation; review translations with fluent speakers.

## 4. Map and timeline

Maps must display data quality and uncertainty where relevant. Exact sensitive locations may be restricted or generalised. A map is not the only way to understand a record; provide list and detail views.

Timelines must distinguish event-start, observation, submission, receipt, acquisition, sampling, processing and intervention dates.

## 5. Case detail

A case workspace should include:
- Evidence-grounded summary.
- Current status and owner.
- Map and geographic context.
- Linked observations and evidence.
- Relationship explanations.
- Timeline.
- Analysis outputs and limitations.
- Competing hypotheses.
- Evidence gaps and next actions.
- Review and audit history.
- Intervention and verification sections when relevant.

## 6. Public information

Public-facing summaries must be approved and must not reveal reporter identity, sensitive locations or unsupported allegations. Clearly distinguish reports, measured findings, interpretations and official statements.

## 7. Usability validation

Test with community reporters, field contributors, reviewers, investigators and environmental specialists. Include low-cost mobile devices, limited connectivity, different levels of digital literacy and language needs. Observe task completion and accidental disclosure; do not rely only on developer review.
