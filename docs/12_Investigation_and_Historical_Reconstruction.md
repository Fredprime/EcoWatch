# 12 — Investigation and Historical Reconstruction

**Status:** Observation/case distinction retained; operational workflow proposed.

## 1. Observation versus case

An observation is an original report, measurement, image, dataset record or other evidence item with its own source, time, location, method and limitations.

A case is a managed investigation concerning a suspected environmental event, condition, pressure or impact. A case may evolve as evidence develops. Original observations remain independently traceable.

## 2. Case creation

A case may be created by an authorised user or a controlled workflow when:
- An observation requires investigation.
- Several observations may relate to a common condition.
- A scheduled monitoring process flags a review need.
- An organisation refers a matter.

Automated correlation may suggest case association but must not establish a case conclusion.

## 3. Investigation workflow

1. Define the question being investigated.
2. Identify the earliest known relevant observation, without assuming it marks the start.
3. Review source, date and location precision.
4. Collect relevant historical imagery, measurements, maps, reports and local knowledge.
5. Compare multiple time points and appropriate seasons.
6. Examine alternative explanations and other potential sources.
7. Identify missing evidence and request field verification if needed.
8. Record findings, uncertainty and unresolved questions.
9. Update case status with human rationale.
10. Refer to an authorised body where appropriate.

## 4. Timeline

A case timeline may include:
- Estimated event start.
- First known observation.
- Additional independent observations.
- Submission and server receipt dates.
- Satellite acquisition dates.
- Field inspection dates.
- Laboratory/sample dates.
- Case status changes.
- Intervention milestones.
- Verification assessments.

Every timeline item must show what its date represents and its source.

## 5. Conflicting accounts

Preserve disagreement. The case summary should distinguish:
- Points of agreement.
- Conflicting claims.
- Firsthand and secondhand sources.
- Independent and dependent evidence.
- Measurements and interpretations.
- Unknowns and evidence gaps.

Do not force one narrative when the evidence does not support it.

## 6. Candidate case statuses

- New / unreviewed.
- Initial review.
- Evidence gathering.
- Corroborated evidence available.
- Field verification required.
- Field verified.
- Referred to responsible authority.
- Under intervention.
- Monitoring outcomes.
- Recovery verified against criteria.
- Not substantiated.
- Closed.
- Merged into another case.

Final status definitions, permissions and public visibility require stakeholder approval. “Field verified” and “recovery verified” must never be assigned by AI alone.

## 7. Sensitive cases

Allegations involving individuals, organisations, private property or vulnerable reporters require restricted access and careful language. Public summaries must not expose identities or present unverified allegations as fact.
