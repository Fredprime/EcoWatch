# 13 — Prioritisation and Decision Support

**Status:** Lifecycle stage approved; scoring criteria and authority model open.

## 1. Purpose

Prioritisation helps authorised people allocate limited investigation and response resources. It is not an automatic judgement of legal liability, environmental severity or public danger.

## 2. Candidate factors

Depending on the use case, decision-makers may consider:
- Potential magnitude and extent of environmental change.
- Persistence or rate of change.
- Sensitivity of the affected ecosystem.
- Exposure of communities or livelihoods, where appropriately assessed.
- Evidence quality and confidence.
- Reversibility or urgency.
- Connectivity to downstream or adjacent areas.
- Data gaps and need for verification.
- Feasibility and authority for response.

Not every factor applies to every case. Criteria and weights must be selected with domain experts and stakeholders.

## 3. Avoid false precision

A single numerical score may hide value judgements and weak evidence. Prefer a transparent factor view, evidence completeness, uncertainty and reviewer rationale. If a ranking score is introduced, document its method, weights, missing-data treatment and validation.

## 4. Human authority

The platform may recommend review order or highlight cases. Authorised people decide priority, referrals, intervention and communication. The system must record who made the decision and why.

## 5. Prioritisation record

Record:
- Case and assessment date.
- Method/version.
- Factors considered.
- Factor values and sources.
- Missing or uncertain inputs.
- Result/rank, if used.
- Human decision and rationale.
- Reviewer and review date.

## 6. Fairness and coverage

Areas with better connectivity or more active reporters may generate more records. Report volume must not be treated as a direct measure of environmental severity. Monitor coverage gaps and avoid systematically deprioritising less connected communities.
