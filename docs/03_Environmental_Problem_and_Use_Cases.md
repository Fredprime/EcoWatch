# 03 — Environmental Problem and Use Cases

**Status:** Broad domain approved; specific pilot problem and location open.

## 1. Connected environmental systems

EcoWatch's long-term scope covers land, water and interconnected ecosystems. A pressure in one location may influence other locations through runoff, sediment transport, drainage, rivers, groundwater, habitat connectivity, wind, or human activity. Which pathway matters depends on local conditions and must be established with suitable evidence.

A possible investigation chain is:
1. Land disturbance or vegetation removal is observed.
2. Rainfall and surface conditions may mobilise sediment or other materials.
3. A drainage or river network may transport material.
4. Downstream water or habitat conditions may change.
5. Communities or livelihoods may report effects.
6. Field measurements and specialist assessment test the proposed relationship.
7. An intervention is planned and its outcomes monitored.

This is a hypothesis framework, not a claim that each step occurs in every location.

## 2. Why one data type is insufficient

- Satellite imagery may show land-cover change but not necessarily its cause.
- Water measurements may show a condition but not independently identify its source.
- Community reports can identify leads and lived experience but are not laboratory measurements.
- Sensors provide repeated readings but depend on calibration, placement and maintenance.
- Agency documents may carry authority but still have scope, date and method limitations.
- Restoration records show activities but do not by themselves establish ecological recovery.

EcoWatch must preserve these differences while enabling cross-source investigation.

## 3. Candidate use cases

### A. Land disturbance and downstream water condition
Combine land-cover observations, watershed/drainage context, rainfall, field reports and upstream/downstream measurements to investigate a possible relationship.

### B. River pollution reports
Collect community observations, field measurements, laboratory results and inspection records; group related evidence; record competing explanations and follow-up sampling.

### C. Erosion and sediment movement
Track disturbed land, exposed soil, rainfall context, erosion observations and receiving water conditions.

### D. Habitat and vegetation change
Compare time-series imagery, field observations and habitat records while accounting for seasonality and sensor limitations.

### E. Restoration monitoring
Record a baseline, intervention activities, repeated measurements and outcome assessments. Distinguish completed work from demonstrated recovery.

These are candidate use cases. The pilot must select a manageable subset.

## 4. Evidence and causal reasoning

An association in time or space is a reason to investigate, not proof of causation. For a suspected land-to-water impact, relevant evidence may include:
- Location and extent of the suspected pressure.
- Catchment boundaries and flow direction.
- Rainfall and hydrological conditions.
- Upstream and downstream measurements.
- Sampling dates, methods, units and laboratory quality controls.
- Other possible sources.
- Historical imagery and field observations.
- Appropriate reference sites where feasible.

## 5. Baselines and environmental variability

A baseline must specify its period, geography, indicator, method and data quality. It is not automatically the oldest available image or first report. Comparisons should account for season, rainfall, sensor quality, cloud cover, measurement methods and natural variability.

## 6. Pilot problem statement

Working formulation:

“In a selected Ghanaian pilot area, environmental observations and historical information may be distributed across different sources. EcoWatch will test whether a traceable evidence and case workflow can organise those observations, identify reviewable relationships, support selected environmental analysis and prioritisation, and track the outcomes of an agreed intervention where partners and suitable measurements are available.”

## 7. Pilot evidence gaps

- Which watershed, district or bounded landscape is suitable?
- Which environmental pressure is documented?
- Which indicators can be measured repeatedly?
- Which local experts can review interpretations?
- Which community and institutional partners are willing to participate?
- Which data can be accessed and reused?
- Are safe field access and intervention opportunities available?
