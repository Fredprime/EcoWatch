# 10 — Intervention and Recovery Management

**Status:** Required by the five-stage vision; detailed operational partners and workflows remain open.

## 1. Purpose

EcoWatch should help track what happens after an environmental problem is identified. It should record response actions and assess outcomes without claiming authority to carry out enforcement or restoration itself.

## 2. Types of response

Depending on the location and responsible institutions, response activities may include:
- Additional field investigation or sampling.
- Referral to a competent authority.
- Containment or mitigation.
- Waste removal or site clean-up.
- Erosion control or land rehabilitation.
- Replanting or habitat restoration.
- Water treatment or alternative water provision.
- Community engagement and risk communication.
- Long-term ecological monitoring.

These examples are not instructions to undertake work without qualified assessment, legal authority and safety planning.

## 3. Intervention record

A response record should include:
- Intervention identifier.
- Related case or cases.
- Objective and rationale.
- Responsible organisation or authorised lead.
- Location and affected area.
- Planned start and completion dates.
- Actual activity dates.
- Resources or dependencies where appropriate.
- Status and milestones.
- Supporting records and evidence.
- Intended environmental outcomes.
- Risks and limitations.

## 4. Activity completion versus environmental recovery

These are different:
- **Activity completed:** the planned task was carried out.
- **Output delivered:** an immediate product exists, such as an area treated or waste removed.
- **Environmental outcome:** an indicator changed in the intended direction.
- **Recovery:** a sustained improvement is supported by appropriate monitoring.

EcoWatch must not equate a completed clean-up with a recovered river or a completed planting activity with a restored ecosystem.

## 5. Outcome indicators

Indicators must be selected for the specific intervention and site. Potential examples include:
- Water-quality measures using consistent methods.
- Vegetation cover or survival over time.
- Erosion or exposed-soil indicators.
- Habitat condition.
- Species observations using appropriate survey methods.
- Continued community access to relevant environmental resources.

A qualified environmental specialist should define sampling design, thresholds, seasonal comparison and interpretation.

## 6. Before-and-after evaluation

A credible assessment should consider:
- Baseline measurements before intervention.
- Consistent post-intervention measurements.
- Suitable time intervals.
- Seasonal and rainfall effects.
- Reference or comparison locations where appropriate.
- Measurement uncertainty and quality controls.
- Other activities that may influence the result.

Where a controlled comparison is not feasible, the report must explain that limitation and avoid overstating causal impact.

## 7. Responsibilities and approvals

EcoWatch may support tracking and reporting. It does not replace the legal or professional authority of environmental agencies, landowners, laboratories, engineers or restoration specialists.

Only authorised users should approve intervention records or mark official verification statuses.

## 8. Recovery status

Possible statuses:
- Proposed.
- Approved.
- Scheduled.
- In progress.
- Activity completed.
- Outcome monitoring.
- Improvement observed.
- Recovery verified against defined criteria.
- No improvement detected.
- Deterioration detected.
- Assessment inconclusive.

The term “recovery verified” should only be used when agreed criteria and evidence support it.

## 9. Learning loop

Verification findings should inform future prioritisation, restoration planning and environmental analysis. If an intervention fails or produces limited improvement, EcoWatch should record the result and the evidence rather than hide it.
