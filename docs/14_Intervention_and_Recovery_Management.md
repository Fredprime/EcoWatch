# 14 — Intervention and Recovery Management

**Status:** Connected lifecycle approved; specific partner roles and procedures open.

## 1. Purpose

EcoWatch records and supports the management of response activities after a case is investigated and an action is authorised. It does not itself confer legal authority or replace qualified environmental, engineering or restoration professionals.

## 2. Candidate interventions

Depending on site conditions and authority:
- Additional field investigation or sampling.
- Referral to a competent authority.
- Containment or mitigation.
- Waste removal or site clean-up.
- Erosion control or land rehabilitation.
- Replanting or habitat restoration.
- Water treatment or alternative water provision.
- Community engagement and risk communication.
- Long-term ecological monitoring.

These examples are not field instructions. Site-specific assessment, safety planning, permits and qualified oversight may be required.

## 3. Intervention record

Include:
- Intervention ID and linked case(s).
- Objective and rationale.
- Responsible organisation and authorised lead.
- Approval/reference.
- Location and affected area.
- Planned and actual dates.
- Activities, milestones and dependencies.
- Resources where appropriate.
- Supporting evidence.
- Intended outcomes and indicators.
- Risks, limitations and follow-up schedule.

## 4. Distinguish four outcomes

1. **Activity completed:** planned work was carried out.
2. **Output delivered:** an immediate product exists, such as waste removed or area treated.
3. **Environmental outcome:** a selected indicator changed in the intended direction.
4. **Recovery:** sustained improvement is supported by suitable monitoring and agreed criteria.

A completed clean-up does not prove a river has recovered. A planting activity does not prove a habitat has been restored.

## 5. Verification design

A credible evaluation should consider:
- Baseline before intervention.
- Consistent post-intervention measurements.
- Appropriate monitoring intervals.
- Seasonal and rainfall effects.
- Reference/comparison locations where suitable.
- Measurement uncertainty and quality controls.
- Other activities that may influence outcomes.
- Qualified interpretation.

If causal attribution cannot be established, report observed change and limitations without claiming the intervention caused it.

## 6. Candidate statuses

Proposed, approved, scheduled, in progress, activity completed, outcome monitoring, improvement observed, recovery verified, no improvement, deterioration, inconclusive or cancelled.

The term “recovery verified” requires approved criteria and evidence. It cannot be a default status after task completion.

## 7. Learning loop

Verification findings should inform future prioritisation and restoration planning. Failed or ineffective interventions must remain visible as learning records.
